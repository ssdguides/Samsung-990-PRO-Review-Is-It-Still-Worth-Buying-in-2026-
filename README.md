The Samsung 990 PRO is still one of the most complete PCIe 4.0 SSDs on the market. It pairs a dedicated DRAM cache with Samsung’s mature controller and firmware. In late 2026 the real question isn’t whether it’s fast. It’s whether the price makes sense, because SSD prices have risen sharply this year.

Key specs

Interface: PCIe 4.0 x4, NVMe 2.0, M.2 2280
Rated speeds: up to 7,450 MB/s read, 6,900 MB/s write
Endurance: 600 TBW (1TB), 1,200 TBW (2TB), 2,400 TBW (4TB)
Warranty: 5 years or the TBW rating, whichever comes first

What those numbers mean in practice
Sequential speed matters for long, continuous transfers like copying a large video project. Day-to-day computing is mostly small, scattered requests, so you’ll notice little difference from a good PCIe 3.0 drive when opening apps. Gaming gains show up in installs, patches and load times, not frame rates.

Why DRAM matters
DRAM holds the drive’s lookup map. It isn’t extra storage, but it helps keep performance consistent under heavy mixed workloads, like game updates while you edit video.

Cooling and compatibility

Desktop with a motherboard M.2 heatsink: use the board’s heatsink.
Gaming laptop: the bare drive is usually the right fit.
PS5: works, but you need a compatible heatsink and must check Sony’s size limits first.
PCIe 3.0 slot: it works, but you won’t get the full speed, so a cheaper drive is the smarter spend.

The 2026 pricing twist
A US report on 2 October 2026 found the 990 PRO 2TB at $389.99, with the PCIe 5.0 Samsung 9100 PRO 2TB only $10 higher. When a faster drive costs nearly the same, compare before you decide.

Verdict: buy it if the price is competitive and you have a Gen4 slot or a PS5. Choose a cheaper drive for browsing and office work, and consider PCIe 5.0 if your board supports it and the price gap is small.
